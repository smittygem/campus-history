The Pit is a basketball arena at the University of New Mexico in Albuquerque. It opened in 1966 and is known for its underground design. The arena has hosted major basketball events, including the 1983 NCAA Final Four. Today, it remains an important part of UNM's history.

## Historical Background and Construction

The Pit was built in 1966 because UNM needed a larger basketball facility. Coach Bob King helped increase the popularity of basketball at the university during the 1960s. The team previously played at Johnson Gym.

The arena officially opened on December 1, 1966. Construction cost approximately $1.4 million. It originally held 14,831 spectators. The building was originally called University Arena.

## Architectural Innovation and Cultural Significance

The Pit has a unique underground design. Its basketball court is approximately 37 feet below ground level. Architect Joe Boehning designed the building.

The seating sections are steep and close to the court. This helps create a loud atmosphere during basketball games. The arena became a popular location for students and Albuquerque residents.

## National Recognition and Athletic Achievement

The Pit was expanded in 1975 to accommodate more spectators. In 1983, it hosted the NCAA Men's Basketball Final Four.

North Carolina State defeated the University of Houston in the championship game. Lorenzo Charles scored the winning basket in the final seconds. The game became an important moment in college basketball history.

Hosting the tournament brought national attention to Albuquerque and UNM.

## Modernization and Lasting Legacy

The Pit underwent major renovations in 2010. These improvements included updated seating, luxury suites, and modern facilities. The arena maintained its original underground design.

Today, The Pit continues to host UNM basketball games. It remains an important landmark for the university and the Albuquerque community.
